# Evidence and resource feasibility

**Status:** scene-specific for the observed observation-budget bottleneck. The reserve/preflight policy below is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated sufficient budget.

**Applicability:** Staged manipulation with separate limits for reviews, synthesis/supervision, Jev calls, native steps, checkpoint bytes and checkpoint keys.

**Inputs and frames/units:** Current remaining counters; documented cost of each operation; planned nominal and recovery reviews; bounded lease calls/native steps; payload/key capacity; pending effects and approved physical scope. Counts are not interchangeable; frame count is not elapsed time.

**Jev versus program:** Planner/program allocates resources and checks whether effects can be verified. Reviewer answers scoped evidence questions. Jev consumes bounded control resources but cannot replace missing observation or persistence capacity.

## Planning rules
1. Budget the whole remaining effect chain, not just travel. Count geometry acquisition, intermediate attachment checks, release, withdrawal, settling comparison, bounded alternatives and outer supervision.
2. Include method-generation and supervision costs when they share the review budget. Do not assume only explicit in-program reviews consume it.
3. Reserve key/byte capacity and a supported persistence path before generating large review outputs.
4. For each failed gate, name the exact missing constraint and the genuinely different evidence or candidate that can resolve it. Do not repeat the same temporal question, occluded features or equivalent samples.
5. Distinguish numerical infeasibility, missing measurement, faulty gate, persistence error and service error. More robot motion does not repair every class.
6. After repeated unproductive revisions, reconsider the plan or evidence strategy rather than only changing stage names. Preserve prior rejections and acknowledged physical progress.
7. Before irreversible or difficult-to-recover actions, require resources for their independent effects. A partial prefix may be planned only within explicit scope and with a safe supervision/handoff decision; it must not be labeled stage completion.

Episode 1 established acquisition with substantial control resources remaining, then exhausted the GPT budget while containment stayed unexecuted. After retention, 135 Jev calls and 405 native steps remained; later containment failures did not consume those motion resources. This shows a resource mismatch, not proof that any particular reserve would have solved placement.

The final authored recovery attempted to preserve one outer review and stop after a limited prefix, but failed before the prefix. Its reserve policy was not successfully exercised.

**Failure/stopping conditions:** Stop before committing to an effect that cannot be measured with available resources. Preserve current command/state and request supervision; do not use budget pressure to lower geometry margins, omit final checks or promote a partial stage to success.

**Evidence IDs:** N-SUM, N-PLAN, N-J1 through N-J10, N-M10; E-SUM and E-P retain the separate service-interruption boundary.
