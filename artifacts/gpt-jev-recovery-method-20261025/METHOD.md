## Jev protocol

The existing seven explicit Jev questions remain unchanged. For each world translation or world rotation-vector axis, negative means decrease, positive means increase, and hold means within the host-provided tolerance. Each question identifies its exact axis selector. The gripper question selects the requested open, close or keep objective. Actual choices are dispatched unchanged by api.control, including every api.drive update.

Python computes pad-center targets, xyzw orientations, finger offsets, partial-face geometry, componentwise bounds, nominal and bounded effect margins, current hand/object motion errors and the release refinement. Jev receives host proprioception and residuals plus the existing phase, label and explicitly historical object evidence. It performs finite direction choices, not image interpretation or metric calculations. Ordinary leases retain 0.02 m translation, 0.08 rad rotation, three native steps and 0.0025 m position tolerance; the single release refinement may use 0.0008 m position tolerance. Reached, moved, stalled and lease exhaustion retain their existing explicit handling.

All added visual reviews use declared structured fields. They distinguish physical identity, correspondence, released support relation, hand separation, orientation change, visibility, external obstruction and whether a specific upward view is informative and locally supported. Final marker definitions are physical identities, not screen-relative labels. A view change does not count as motion or stability evidence; current calibrated world measurements are compared. Neither successful pose control, closure delivery nor a track alone establishes an object effect.
