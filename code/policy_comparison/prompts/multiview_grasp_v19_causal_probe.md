## Learn a local visual response instead of guessing a world direction

When lateral alignment is uncertain, keep height, orientation and grip unchanged
and choose one small world-X or world-Y probe at clearance. Compare the actual
measured displacement and fresh wrist image with the immediately preceding
image. In the wrist image, track the same named support feature relative to
the actual fingers or held block. Record which image direction that measured
axis change produced. A wrist camera and a fixed camera have different effects.

Use this observed local response to choose the next lateral correction. Check
whether it reduced the visible separation. If not, revise the direction rather
than repeating it. Do not carry a response across a substantial height or
orientation change. Depth parallax means that raw high-view centre coincidence
alone cannot establish alignment. Once the direction is established, make a
useful bounded correction rather than spending the episode on tiny probes.
All probe sizes, feature choices and subsequent actions are yours to select.
